# Audit dossiers — evidence that checks itself

> `Vapor.Audit`, `mix vapor.audit`, the console's *Dossier* panel. Since 0.8.0.

## The pain

Whoever has to show an auditor (or a regulator, or a customer) how an AI
system was built and how it behaved gathers, today, screenshots,
spreadsheets and PDFs exported by hand. None of it proves anything: a PDF
does not say whether it was edited, a spreadsheet does not say where the
numbers came from, and the auditor has no way to redo the calculation. vapor
already produces evidence that **checks itself**: the certificate of each
compilation (the six-rung ladder, signed), the journal of each agent run
(Merkle chain, Ed25519 attestation), the transparency-log receipts, the
quality report with controls, the contract of each model admitted by the
airlock. What was missing was an envelope.

## What a dossier is

A `.vdossier` file (canonical CBOR, no clock inside: the same evidence and
the same date give the same bytes) with:

- a **manifest**: for each item, the type, the name, the size, the SHA-256,
  the summary of its verification and **the clauses it is relevant to**;
- the **items**, byte for byte as the system wrote them;
- a **Merkle root** (RFC 6962) over the items;
- **Ed25519 signatures** over the canonical manifest — as many as wanted
  (whoever assembled it, a witness, the technical lead), with a quorum at
  verification;
- optional **anchors** in a transparency log (`Vapor.Tlog`): the dossier
  comes to exist publicly at a date, and cannot be swapped later without
  the swap showing.

```sh
mix vapor.audit keygen chave
mix vapor.audit export --out sistema.vdossier --key chave --cosign testemunha \
    --system "assistente-juridico" --version 1.4.0 --provider ACME \
    --certificate build/cert.bin --journal runs/42.journal --attestation runs/42.att.json \
    --quality docs/bench/quality.json --model /modelos/qwen2-7b \
    --document avaliacao-de-risco.md:AIA-11,ISO42001-A.6.2.3 --html --pdf
mix vapor.audit verify sistema.vdossier --trusted chave.pub --quorum 1   # exits 1 if anything fails, saying what
mix vapor.audit demo                                                     # a complete dossier of this checkout
```

## Three ways to check, none requires trusting vapor

1. **`mix vapor.audit verify`** (or `Vapor.Audit.verify/2`): recomputes each
   hash, the root, the signatures (only `--trusted` keys count), the quorum,
   the anchors (with `--log-key`) and **each item by its own rules** — the
   certificate by its own signatures, the journal by the chain and the
   attestation, the receipt by the inclusion proof and the signed checkpoint,
   the quality report by all of its checks having passed.
2. **The HTML page** (`--html`): the whole dossier inside a page that checks
   itself **in the browser, offline** — a CBOR reader in JavaScript, SHA-256
   and Ed25519 from WebCrypto. Opening the file is enough; nothing is fetched
   from the network. One altered byte turns the page red and says which item.
3. **The PDF report** (`--pdf`): for printing and filing; the text lists the
   evidence and the root, and the `.vdossier` goes **attached**
   (`/EmbeddedFiles`) — `pdfdetach` extracts it, and `verify` accepts the PDF
   itself.

In the console, the **Dossier** panel checks a file dropped there and draws
the **grid**: provisions in the rows, evidence in the columns, a filled cell
where the type of evidence is relevant, red where the item failed, and on the
right how many verified items support each provision — **a gap shows up as a
gap** ("none"), it does not vanish. The "Build a demonstration dossier"
button does what `mix vapor.audit demo` does and offers the `.vdossier` and
the self-verifying page for download.

## The clause mapping is data, not opinion

| type of evidence | AI Act (EU) | ISO/IEC 42001 |
|---|---|---|
| compilation certificate | Art. 15; Annex IV §2(g) | A.6.2.4 |
| attested agent journal | Art. 12, Art. 19 | A.6.2.8 |
| transparency-log receipt | Art. 12, Art. 19 | A.6.2.8 |
| quality report (with controls) | Art. 15; Annex IV §2(g), §4 | A.6.2.4 |
| model contract (airlock) | Art. 11, Art. 13; Annex IV §2(b) | A.6.2.7 |
| merge receipt | Art. 11 | A.6.2.3 |
| document (risk assessment, etc.) | Art. 11 (or whatever is declared) | A.6.2.7 (or whatever is declared) |

`Vapor.Audit.clauses/0` and `mapping/0` are the table; an item can declare
its own (`--document path:CLAUSE,…`). The grid shows, for example, that the
evidence generated by the system **does not cover** ISO/IEC 42001 A.6.2.3
(design and development documentation) without a human document — it is the
kind of gap an auditor looks for, and it is better that the dossier shows it
first.

## What a dossier is not

**It is not a conformity assessment, a certification or a legal opinion.** A
dossier authenticates evidence: it proves that these bytes came out of this
system, in this form, signed by these keys, and that each item passes its
own rules. Whether the evidence is *sufficient* for an article of the AI Act
is a legal question the dossier does not answer — it makes it answerable.
The notice goes inside every dossier, in the PDF and on the page.

## How it is tested

- `test/vapor/audit_dossier_test.exs`: assemble, sign, co-sign, anchor;
  check offline; **any altered byte is rejected, and the report says
  where** (an item: the hash; the manifest: the signatures; an item swapped
  with the hash updated in the manifest: the signatures); an untrusted
  signer does not count; the PDF gives back the dossier (and poppler's
  `pdftotext`/`pdfdetach` agree); the HTML page checks in a browser engine
  (Node's WebCrypto) and turns red with an altered item.
- `mix vapor.quality` §5d: 0 of ~200 one-bit alterations spread across the
  file accepted (control: the altered files themselves).
- `test/vapor/console_test.exs`: the demonstration dossier checks through the
  console; the same with one byte changed does not.
